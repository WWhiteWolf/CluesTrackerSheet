This app is here to help you enjoy and win the classic deduction board games you already play.

It is a companion for the real board on the real table. You tell it what to write, and it keeps the notes, freeing you to devote your mind to the clues and the facts.

This Guide leads you through the game itself as it develops — getting you set up, and showing you the turns.

The app arrives dark. If you would rather read it light, one tap of ☀ or 🌙 changes it.

Setting up

Setup asks about ten minutes of you the first game, and then never asks again. Nothing here is hard — you just step your way down the screen.

  1. Players. Tap a seat, type a name, and tap Confirm. Fill them in the order people will play. When you come to your own seat, check "This is Me" before you tap Confirm — and don't worry about a name for yourself.
  2. Cards. This is the part that takes the time. Tap Rename → Edit in a category banner and give the categories and cards the names that match your game. Then tap Save — nothing is kept until you do.
  3. Save Card Deck. Tap it and give the deck a name. That is your ten minutes done for good. Later, with a tap you can load the deck you want.
  4. Where are the cards? Tap it and tell the app how many cards each player was dealt. Then tap Done.
  5. Your hand. Tap the chips for the cards you are holding, and they turn green as you do.

And that is all of it. ▶ Play brightens at the top right, tap it and you are playing. If it is still dim, tap it anyway and it will tell you, at the bottom of the screen, what is missing.

Each of these is explained in full in Setup, below, whenever you want it.

Playing the game
How the Game page is laid out

A few things sit around the sheet, in the same place all game. Each is explained in full further down.

  • The header runs across the top. New at the left starts a fresh game, and Out at the right is for a player who leaves the table. Both have their own sub-section later.
  • The turn bar sits above the sheet and says whose turn it is. At its right are ⭐ Accuse, for when someone names their solution, and the ☀ / 🌙 button that swaps the dark screen for the light one.
  • The hint bar below it says what to do next. ↩ Undo waits at its left, and clears the whole turn so you can run it again.
  • Next ▶ stands tall at the right of both bars, and ends the turn.
  • The footer runs across the bottom, holding ← Log, 📖 Guide and Notes →.

Tap ▶ Play and the sheet appears — the cards down the left, the players across the top, and a box wherever the two meet. You have seen it before. What has changed is that you no longer hold the pencil: you say what happened at the table, and the app does the writing. The marks fill themselves in as you go, and there is nothing to memorise.

Two bars sit above the sheet. The upper one says whose turn it is. The lower one says what to do next. Look up from the table having lost your place, and those two lines give it back.

Every turn then goes the same way.

  1. Asking. Tap the three cards the player asked for, in their own column. The app counts them as you go, and moves on by itself when you tap the third.
  2. When someone shows. Tap the name at the top of that player's column. If you were the one who showed, or the one who asked, a pop-up asks which card — tap it and tap Confirm.
  3. When nobody shows. Tap Next ▶, and every player but the asker is crossed off all three cards at once.
  4. Ending the turn. Tap Next ▶ and the next player is up.

And that is the turn. Every one after it runs the same way.

Tapped something wrong? ↩ Undo takes two taps and clears the whole turn, so you can run it again.

Each of these is explained in full in The Game screen and Playing a turn, below, whenever you want it.

SETUP

You shape the deck to match the game on your table, and you can save it — the app keeps up to four decks, ready to load next time.

It all happens on one screen, and you work your way down it. The screen marks its own two steps as you go.

STEP 1 - PLAYERS is the six seats at the top, one for each person at the table.

STEP 2 - CARDS is your deck below it — the categories and their cards — with two buttons sitting above them, one for saving the deck you build, and one for telling the app how the cards were dealt. In each category banner sits a Rename → Edit button that lets you rename the categories and cards.

Along the bottom are ← Log, 📖 Guide and Notes →. The Log and the Notepad each have a section of their own further down, and 📖 Guide is what you are reading now.

That strip is also where the app speaks: a message appears across it for a few seconds, hiding those buttons, and then clears itself and gives them back. A long message slides past rather than being cut short.

That is the whole screen. Here is each piece of it in turn.

Players:

Player seats sit at the top, and from two to six of them can be filled.

  • Tap one to open it, type a name of up to six characters, and tap Confirm.
  • Enter every player's name this way except your own.
  • Fill the seats in the order people will play — the game takes turns in seat order, Seat 1 first.
  • On your own seat, check "This is Me" before you Confirm. Your seat shows Me, so you can always find yourself — and you don't need to fill in your name.
Cards:

Your deck is the cards inside the categories, and it starts out as placeholders.

  • The app arrives with three categories of nine cards — Category 1 to 3, Card 1 to Card 27 — waiting to become your deck for the game.
  • A deck holds up to thirty cards in all.
  • No two cards can share a name.

This is the ten minutes. It is the one part the app cannot do for you, because only you can see the box.

Categories:

A category is one group of cards, and it gets its own banner down the left of the sheet later.

  • The app starts you with three, and you can have up to four.
  • ＋ Add Category sits below the last one and disappears once you have four. A new category arrives empty, and you name it and fill it using Rename → Edit.
  • To remove one, open it with Rename → Edit and tap the ✕ beside its name, which takes two taps. Its cards leave your hand with it.
  • One category always has to stay, so the app refuses to delete the last.
  • A category you empty of every card removes itself when you Save.
Rename → Edit:

Tap Rename → Edit in a category banner to open it. Inside you can:

  • give the category its real name
  • give each card its real name
  • remove a card with the ✕ beside it
  • add one with ＋ Add Card

Names run to thirteen characters, categories and cards alike, so a long one will need shortening.

Nothing changes until you tap Save. Cancel leaves the category exactly as it was, and Close does the same.

Save Card Deck:

When the whole deck matches your game, it can be saved and loaded back another time.

  • Tap 💾 Save Card Deck and give it a short name — six characters. A chip with that name appears just above the cards.
  • You can keep up to four decks.
  • Next time, tap the chip and then Load, and the ten minutes is already saved.
  • Delete on the same pop-up throws a deck away. Load and Delete both take two taps.

A saved deck holds the categories and cards and nothing else, so the app will still ask where the cards are each time you play.

Where are the cards?:

Before a game can start, the app needs to know how the deck was dealt — how many cards each player holds, and how many are left on the board, which the app calls the House. The House cards come from a shortened variation of the game, used to even out the deal between the players. They sit face down on the board.

  • Fill the seats first, since the pop-up will not open without them.
  • Tap Where are the cards? and it opens with a sensible guess already made: an even share to the players, and any leftovers to the House.
  • Use − and ＋ to match the real deal, then tap Done.
  • Once you tap Done, the button itself fills in green, so a glance tells you the app has what it needs.
  • The Envelope row at the top is fixed — one card from each category, set aside as the solution.

How the cards are split is your own choice — you can leave the House empty and give the players uneven numbers. They have to add up exactly, though: the House and the players together holding the whole deck apart from the envelope, since every dealt card is somewhere.

Your hand:

The cards themselves sit below each category banner.

  • Tap every card you are holding and it turns green.
  • Tap it again if you tap one by mistake.
  • The number of green cards has to match the count you gave for your own seat.
▶ Play:

▶ Play waits at the top right.

  • It stays dim until the app has everything it needs.
  • If you tap it while it is dim, it tells you what is still missing. The message appears across the bottom of the screen, over the ← Log, 📖 Guide and Notes → buttons.
  • When it brightens, tap it, and the game begins.
THE GAME SCREEN

Tap ▶ Play and the sheet appears. You have seen it before — it is the paper sheet that comes in the box, now drawn on your phone.

The cards run down the left under their category banners. The players run across the top. The boxes, where a card meets a player, are where what you learn gets written.

None of that has changed. What has changed is who holds the pencil. You tell the app what happened at the table, and it does the writing. Say who showed a card, and every ✗ that follows is filled in for you.

Two columns sit there that the paper never had. The Summary ∑ is yours alone, a place to set down what you have worked out. 🏠 is for the cards no player was dealt, the ones left on the board. Each is explained clearly further on.

The sheet scrolls when there is more of it than fits. The names along the top and the cards down the left stay put while it moves, so you never lose track of which box you are in.

If the columns are not in the order you want, press and hold a name at the top for a moment, then slide it. Only CARDS keeps its place. The app remembers how you left them.

The marks

These are what appear in the boxes as the game goes on. There is nothing to memorise — you will pick them up as you play.

  • ✓ this player has this card
  • ✗ this player does not have this card
  • ? this player asked about this card
  • ! this player showed a card, but you do not know which
  • Me you showed this card to this player

A box can also carry a short note of your own in the corner, which has its own section further on.

Above the sheet

In the header, New sits at the left and Out at the right. Each opens a pop-up that explains itself.

Two bars sit across the top — another part the paper never had.

The upper bar tracks whose turn it is, and how far along it is: "Turn 3 — Sarah is thinking 🤔". ⭐ Accuse sits at the right of that bar, and has a section of its own further on.

The lower bar hints what to do next, in the middle. Both orient you at a glance when you come back from a distraction.

The ↩ Undo button waits at its left and takes two taps. It clears the whole turn and starts it over, so it undoes a wrong move.

The Next ▶ button stands tall at the right of both bars.

The next section, Taking a turn, explains those three — the hint, Undo and Next.

PLAYING A TURN
The turn bar:

The upper bar names whose turn it is and how far along the turn is. When the turn is yours it says You rather than a name.

  • "Turn 3 — Sarah is thinking 🤔" while she is choosing what to ask.
  • "Sarah is waiting ⏳" once the three cards are in and the app is waiting to hear who showed.
  • "showed ✓" once a name has been given.

⭐ Accuse sits at the right of this bar, and has a section of its own further on.

The hint bar:

The lower bar says what the app is waiting for, between the ↩ Undo and Next ▶ buttons. Three of them stand there, one for each stage of a turn, and each stays until the stage changes.

  • "Select 3 cards in Sarah's column" — the ask has not been entered yet.
  • "Tap the name of who showed — or Next ▶" — the ask is in, and the app wants to know what happened.
  • "Tap Next ▶ to continue" — the turn is done and only needs closing.

That same line is where the app speaks to you. A message takes the bar for about five seconds — a warning in its own color — and then the standing hint comes back on its own. A message too long for the width slides across rather than being cut short.

Coming back from a distraction, those two bars together tell you where the table is.

↩ Undo and Next ▶ each have a section of their own further on.

Asking:
  • When a player asks, tap the three cards they named, in their own column — one from each category. A ? appears in each box as you tap it.
  • The app counts as you go and says how far along you are: "2/3 — need: Rooms".
  • When you tap the third card, the turn moves on by itself. Nothing else needs pressing.
Changing a pick:

Until you name who showed, all three picks stay open.

  • Tap a picked card again and the ? comes off it.
  • Tap a different card in the same category and the pick swaps across to it.
  • It does not have to be the one you tapped last — any of the three can be changed.

Once you tap a name at the top of a column, the ask is closed, and at that point ↩ Undo is the only way back, in case you need it.

When a player asks nothing:
  • A player who asks nothing simply passes. Tap Next ▶ with no cards picked at all, and the turn goes to the next player.
  • The Log records the pass.
Half an ask:
  • The app will not take one card, or two. Tap Next ▶ partway through and it refuses the tap and says "Select all or none of the cards in Sarah's column first".
  • Either finish the three, or take the picks back off and pass the turn.
Naming who showed:

With the ask in, the app waits to be told what happened. Tap the name at the top of the column of the player who showed a card. That one tap is the whole of it. What gets written depends on who was involved.

A show between two other players:
  • The card was not shown to you, so you know only that something was shown. The app writes down what is actually known.
  • A ! goes in that player's box for each of the three cards asked — they showed one of these, and you do not know which. Every player sitting between the asker and the shower is ✗-ed on all three, since the ask passed them and they could not show.
  • A box already holding a ✓ or a ✗ is never overwritten.
Someone shows you:
  • When you are the one who asked, you know exactly which card is shown. A pop-up opens — "Sarah shows you…" — listing the asked cards you are not already holding, since those are the only ones she could have shown. Tap the card you were shown, then Confirm. A ✓ goes into Sarah's box.
  • Being shown is the only way a ✓ is ever written. You never mark one yourself.
  • A ✓ settles that card for the whole table: every other player still in the game is ✗-ed on it at once, because only one of them can be holding it.
You show someone:
  • When the card that has to be shown is yours, the pop-up says "You show Sarah…" and lists the cards among the three that are in your hand. Pick the card you want to show — if only one of them is yours it arrives already picked — then tap Confirm. Me goes into the asker's box, a record of what you gave away, and to whom.
Cancel and Close:
  • Either button shuts the pop-up and writes nothing. The turn stays where it was, still waiting to be told who showed.
When nobody shows:
  • If the ask went all the way round and no one could show, tap Next ▶. Every player but the asker is ✗-ed on all three cards, and the app says "No player showed — those cards are ❌-ed out for them".
  • If someone did in fact have to show, the app refuses the tap. The next section says why.
When the app refuses a tap on the name at the top of a column:

Tap a name the app cannot accept and it refuses the tap, and says why on the hint line. There are four.

  • The asker cannot also show. A player does not show a card to themselves.
  • A player already ✗-ed on all three cards has nothing to show.
  • A player who has quit cannot show. Their cards left the game with them.
  • A player earlier in the ring has to show first. If someone between the asker and the name you tapped is already known to hold one of the three, they would have had to show it, and the app names them: "Sarah must show first — they have a confirmed card".
Taking back a wrong name:
  • Tapped the wrong name for a show between two other players? Tap that same name again and confirm. Everything the app wrote when you tapped that name comes off — the ! marks, the
  • ✗ marks of the players passed over, and its line in the Log —

and the turn goes back to waiting for the right name.

  • You can do this until Next ▶ is tapped. It does not work when you were the one who showed, or the one who asked — those go through a pop-up, and ↩ Undo is the way back from them.
↩ Undo:

↩ Undo sits at the left of the hint bar and takes two taps. It does not step back one tap: it clears the whole turn and starts it over.

  • Everything the app wrote that turn on the sheet comes off — the ? marks from the ask, and any ✓, ✗, ! or Me a show put down. A board mark you made with 🏠 during that turn goes with them.
  • A ! can appear where you had not seen one. A ! sitting under a ✓ or a ✗ is held out of sight while that mark is there, so when the mark comes off the ! shows again.
  • The Turn Log keeps its lines and gains one saying the turn was undone.
  • On a turn you have not touched yet, it steps back into the turn before and clears that one instead.
  • Marks the app made when a player quit are left alone, and so are the marks that came from your own hand when the game began. Your notes in the Summary ∑ column stay as well.
Ending the turn:

Tap Next ▶ and the next player is up. Play goes in seat order, around the table as the seats were filled.

SUMMARY COLUMN (∑)

The Summary ∑ column is your own place to keep the conclusion you have reached about each card. The app never fills it in or uses it to make decisions.

HOUSE COLUMN (🏠)

For game versions where undealt cards sit on the board (common with 2 players). When a card from the board is peeked at privately by you, as some versions allow, tap its 🏠 cell — a ✅ appears and the card is crossed off with ❌ in every player's column, since nobody can hold it. Tap the ✅ again to clear it; the ❌s it placed are removed too (any ❌ that was already there stays). Each board mark is noted in the Turn Log.

CELL NOTE

Tap any player column cell — except the column of the player whose turn it is while it's highlighted — to add a short personal note. A popup opens showing the card and player name.

MAKING AN ACCUSATION (⭐)

When any player announces their accusation, tap ⭐ Accuse on the Turn Bar.

What the verdict does:
  • You are right: You won. The Turn Bar celebrates, and the game is over.
  • You are wrong: You take no more turns and no longer need the app, but do not turn in your cards. Stay involved and show a card when asked.
  • They are right: The game is over, and the Turn Bar names the winner.
  • They are wrong: They take no more turns, but stay at the table to show cards. Their header fades and shows 😢. If it was their turn, the next turn begins immediately. If no other opponent can take a turn, the game is over and you win; otherwise, play continues.

Every accusation writes a permanent Turn Log entry naming the accuser, the cards and the verdict. No verdict can be undone.

PLAYER OUT

When a player walks away from the game, they must turn over their cards for everyone to see. Tap Out, then fill in the pop-up.

Marks placed by a Player Out are permanent — Undo never removes them — and the Turn Log gets a permanent entry. There is no way back from Out. If a player who is out on a wrong accusation (😢 — see MAKING AN ACCUSATION above) later walks away too, tap Out and mark them 👋 Quits.

New

Tap New to start a new game. A popup appears — choose your players, choose your deck, then start:

TURN LOG

Tap ← Log from the Game screen to view a record of every turn — who asked, which cards, and who showed or if nobody showed. The log clears when a new game is started.

NOTEPAD

Tap Notes → from the Game or Setup screen to open a free-text notepad. Type anything you want to remember. Notes are saved automatically and persist across games and also after closing and opening the app again. Tap Clear (two taps) to erase all notes.

Seat

Every one of the six player seats on the Setup page opens the same pop-up. It is where a player gets their name, and where you mark which seat is yours.

Opening it:
  • Tap any of the six slots at the top of the Setup page. The title bar names the one you tapped, Seat 1 through Seat 6.
  • A seat that already has a name opens with that name waiting, ready to be changed.
Naming a player:
  • Type the name, up to six characters.
  • The slot shows the name once you Confirm.
Marking your own seat:
  • Tap "This is Me". The name box empties as you tap it — you do not type your own name at all.
  • The slot reads Me from then on.
  • Only one seat can be yours. Marking a different seat as Me moves it there and leaves the old one empty.
Emptying a seat:
  • Confirm with the name box empty and "This is Me" unchecked, and the slot goes back to a dash.
Confirm:
  • Confirm applies what the pop-up is showing and closes it.
  • Nothing reaches the Setup page until you tap it.
Clear:
  • Clear empties the name box and unchecks "This is Me" in one tap.
＋ Add Category

The app arrives with three categories, and some games are played with four. ＋ Add Category is how you add the one you are missing.

Adding one:

Look below the last category on the Setup page, underneath its row of cards.

  • The button reads ＋ Add Category (up to 4), and a single tap adds the category.
  • Four is as many as the app will hold. Once you have four the button is no longer there, so there is nothing to tap by mistake.
The new category:

A new category appears at the end of the list, and it holds no cards.

  • It arrives under a name the app supplies, Category 4, with a zero beside it until you put cards in.
  • Edit, beside that name, is where you give it the name your game uses and add its cards.
The card counts:

Adding a category unsettles the counts, even though it brings no cards with it.

  • The green comes off Where are the cards? the moment the category is added.
  • You will need to confirm the counts again, and you may need to re-enter the card counts themselves — ▶ Play will not wake until you have.
Edit

Edit sits at the right of each category's line on the Setup page, and it opens as a pop-up carrying that category's name in its title bar. This is where a category and its cards get their real names, and where cards are added and taken out.

Naming:
  • The top box names the category, and each box below names one card. They cannot be more than thirteen characters each.
  • A name showing in grey is a starting name the app supplied. You can type over it, or leave it as is.
  • A box you leave alone keeps the name it is showing, so you can rename one card and leave the rest.
Deleting the category:
  • The ✕ beside the category name deletes the category and every card in it.
  • It takes two taps. The first turns the ✕ into Delete? and the pop-up's line reads "Tap again to confirm"; the second does it and the pop-up closes.
  • Leave Delete alone for about three seconds and it goes back to ✕, as though the first tap had never happened.
  • One category always has to stay, so the app refuses to delete the last one.
Adding and removing cards:

Nothing here reaches your deck until you tap Save, so you can shape the list freely first.

  • ＋ Add Card puts an empty box at the foot of the list with the cursor already in it. A new box left empty is ignored.
  • The ✕ beside a card takes its row off the list straight away. The card leaves the deck when you Save.
  • A card you rename stays in the deck under its new name.
Save:

Save applies the whole pop-up at once — the category's name, every card name, and every card you added or took out.

  • Two things stop it, and when either does, nothing at all is saved: more than thirty cards in the deck, or a card name already used somewhere in it.
  • Over thirty, and the app says "Maximum 30 cards total — the deck is full".
  • A clash, and it says Duplicate card name with the one it found. Capitals make no difference — a name is taken however it was typed.
  • The message lands on the pop-up's own line and the pop-up stays open, so you can put it right and save again.
  • A category you have emptied of every card removes itself when you Save, as long as another category remains.

Every Save clears the card counts — even a Save with nothing typed in. The Where are the cards? button loses its green, and the counts have to be filled in again before the app will let you play. If you opened the pop-up only to look, leave it without tapping Save.

💾 Save Card Deck

💾 Save Card Deck sits under STEP 2 - CARDS on the Setup page, with Where are the cards? beside it. It opens as a pop-up that files a copy of the deck you have shaped, so you can keep it and load it back another time.

Saving a deck:

There is one box in the pop-up, and naming the deck is the whole of the work.

  • Type a name of up to six characters and tap Save.
  • A chip carrying that name appears just above the cards.
  • Clear empties the box if you want to start the name again. It changes nothing else.
What a saved deck keeps:

A saved deck is a copy taken at the moment you tap Save, and what that means is worth knowing before you need it.

  • It holds the categories and their cards and nothing else. The card counts are not in it, so the app still asks where the cards are each time you play.
  • The copy does not change when your deck does. Rename a card after saving and the saved deck still carries the old name.
  • There is no way to bring a saved deck up to date, because a name already in use is refused. To replace one, delete it first and save again under the same name.
When it will not save:

Three things stop a save, and each says so on the pop-up's own line, between the box and the buttons.

  • An empty box. The app says "Enter a deck name first".
  • Four decks already saved. The app says "Maximum 4 saved decks — delete one first", and deleting one is the only way past it.
  • A name already in use. The app says "That name is already used", and capitals make no difference — a name is taken however it was typed.

Nothing is saved when a save is refused, and the pop-up stays open so you can put it right.

the saved deck chip

Every deck you save puts a chip on the Setup page. The chips sit in a bar of their own directly beneath 💾 Save Card Deck and Where are the cards?, above the cards themselves, and each one carries the name its deck was saved under. There is a chip for each saved deck, up to four of them.

Opening it:
  • Tap the chip. It opens as a pop-up titled Saved Deck, with the deck's own name in quotation marks beneath the title.
  • It offers Load and Delete. There is no way to bring a saved deck up to date from here — to replace one, delete it and save again under the same name.
Loading a deck:

Loading replaces the deck's categories and cards you are working on with the saved ones, it is worth knowing what comes with it.

  • Tap Load twice: The pop-up closes, and the saved deck is placed and the app names the deck and says it was loaded.
  • A saved deck is a copy taken at the moment it was saved, so it arrives as it was when it was saved.
  • Your own hand is unselected, and the card counts are cleared. The app will need Where are the cards? filled again before it lets you play.
  • A category that had no cards in it when the deck was saved does not come back. If that would leave no categories at all, the app puts its own starting ones in instead.
Deleting a deck:
  • Tap Delete twice. The pop-up closes, the chip goes with it, and the app names the deck and says it was deleted.
  • Only the saved copy goes. The deck you are working on is untouched, and deleting is what makes room once four decks are saved.
The two taps:

Load and Delete both act the moment the second tap lands, so the two taps are the confirmation and there is nothing further to agree to.

  • The first tap changes the button to Load? or Delete?, and the pop-up's own line reads "Tap again to confirm".
  • The second tap does it.
  • Leave it about three seconds and the button goes back to its plain wording, as though the first tap had never happened. Closing the pop-up and opening it again clears it as well.
Where are the cards?

Where are the cards? sits in Setup beside 💾 Save Card Deck. It opens as a pop-up asking how the deck was dealt — how many cards each player holds, and how many were left on the board, which the app calls the House.

You already know all of that. The app is the one that cannot see the deal, and it is asking for its own sake: the number you give for your own seat is what it checks your tapped hand against. Tapping cards on a phone is easy to get wrong by one and hard to notice, and once a game has started there is no going back to Setup to put it right.

Filling it in:

The pop-up will not open without the seats being filled first. It opens already filled in with a sensible guess — an even share to each player, the leftovers to the House — so when that is how the cards went, have a glance and tap Done.

  • Envelope is fixed: one card from each category, set aside as the solution. The app states it, you never enter it.
  • Set the House first, then the players. Moving the House resets every player's number to an even share of what is left, so a row you had already set by hand is overwritten. Each player's own − and ＋ move that row alone.
  • Done checks one thing, and it checks it exactly: the House, the players and Envelope together must hold the whole deck. Numbers that do not add up are refused and nothing is kept until they do.
  • Once Done takes, the pop-up closes and the button fills green, so a glance at Setup afterwards tells you the count has what the app needs.
  • Notice a mistake before you play and you can open it again — your numbers come back as you left them.

If honest numbers will not add up, it is worth stopping to resolve the count, finding out here is easier than finding it out from a sheet that has stopped making sense.

If the green goes away:

The numbers describe one particular deal, so when the deal underneath them changes the app lets them go — the green comes off the button and ▶ Play waits until they are entered again.

  • What clears them:
    • a seat change of any kind — a name added, changed or cleared, or Me moved
    • ＋ Add Category, or deleting a category
    • any Save in the Edit pop-up, even one where you typed nothing
    • loading a saved deck
  • What leaves them alone:
    • confirming a seat without having changed anything
    • tapping the card chips in your own hand
  • Numbers you set by hand do not come back. Reopening shows the app's guess again, so an uneven deal has to be set a second time.

A New Game keeping the same players and the same deck is the one road where your numbers survive — the green still comes off, and every game gets its own Done.

The House number here and the 🏠 column on the sheet are not tied together and are not meant to match — see the House column below for why.

▶ Play

One tap on a bright ▶ Play starts the game. There is no confirmation, and Setup closes for that game, so this is the moment for one last look at the seats, the deck, the counts and the cards marked as yours.

What it puts on the sheet:
  • The players and cards from Setup become the game sheet.
  • Your cards begin with ✓ in your column and ✗ for every other player. Every other card begins with ✗ in your column. These are the sheet’s starting marks, and Undo never removes them.
  • Turn 1 opens on the first filled seat, and play follows seat order from there.
Someone showed you

When another player shows you a card, tap their name at the top of the column. The pop-up shows the cards they could have shown you. Tap the one card they showed you, then tap Confirm.

You showed someone

When you show a card to another player, tap Me at the top of your column. The pop-up shows the cards you could have shown. Tap the one card you showed, then tap Confirm.

Next ▶

Next ▶ is the tall button standing at the right of both bars during game play, and it is how one turn ends and the next turn begins. Play goes in seat order, around the table as the seats were filled. One tap does whatever the turn needs at that moment, so what it does depends on where the turn stands.

Ending a finished turn:
  • A turn ends in one of three ways — a name has been tapped, nobody could show, or nothing was able to be asked.
  • On the first two the current player has reached a room and asked the 3 question. The hint line reads "Tap Next ▶ to continue". Tap it, and the next player is up.
  • The third is a pass. The current player did not reach a room and cannot ask the 3 question. Next ▶ ends that turn too, but the hint line still reads "Select 3 cards in Sarah's column", because the app is waiting on an ask that never comes. See
Passing a turn:
  • A player who asks nothing simply passes. Tap Next ▶ with no cards picked at all.
  • The turn goes to the next player, and the Log records the pass.
When nobody could show:
  • The ask is in, it went all the way round the table, and no one could show a card. Tap Next ▶ without tapping any name.
  • Every player but the asker is ✗-ed on all three cards, and the app says "No player showed — those cards are ❌-ed out for them".
  • That same tap ends the turn as well. Next ▶ is not tapped again.
When Next ▶ is refused:

There are two times the app will not take the tap, and each says why on the hint line.

  • One card, or two, picked: The app takes all three or none, and says "Select all or none of the cards in Sarah's column first". Either finish the three, or take the picks back off and pass the turn.
  • Someone had to show. If a player is already known to hold one of the three cards, they cannot pass — they have to show it. The app names them: "Sarah must show first — they have a confirmed card". When that player is you, it reads "You must show first — you have a confirmed card".
When the game is over:
  • Once the game has ended, nothing more can be recorded. Next ▶ is refused, and the app says "Game over — tap New to start a new game".
  • The hint line sits empty until a new game begins.
  • The side pages stay open to you. ← Log, 📖 Guide, and Notes → all still work, so the game can be read back after it ends.
  • A player who is out of the running takes no more turns and is stepped over, but stays in the game as someone who can still
Summary ∑

Tap a cell in the Summary ∑ column. The pop-up names the card and gives you three choices:

  • ✕ Has card means the card has been located — in your hand, another player’s hand, or the House.
  • ⭐ Solution? marks the card as a possible solution.
  • Clear removes your mark from that Summary cell.

Each choice takes effect and closes the pop-up on one tap. There is no Confirm button. Open the cell again if you want to change it. The choice will not be logged until Next is tapped.

If another card in the same category already has a ⭐, the first tap warns you and changes the button to Sure?. A second tap places the other ⭐.

Cell notes

Type up to 4 characters that mean something to you, then tap Save. The note appears in the bottom-right corner of the cell. To remove it, empty the box and tap Save.

Accuse

When the pop-up opens, tap the accuser — you included — then tap the accused cards, one from each category.

The verdict question wakes when the cards are all picked: “Are you right?” for your own accusation, “Are they right?” for anyone else. Yes and No each take two taps. The first changes the button to Sure?; the second records the verdict and closes the pop-up. A verdict is forever, so the app makes sure.

Quit

Tap 👋 Quits. The pop-up opens with the card list; tap each card they held.

A card already ✓ in another player's column is unavailable and names its holder — it cannot be in two hands.

Tap Remove Player twice. The first tap changes it to Sure?; the second removes the player and closes the pop-up.

Their shown cards get ✓ in their column and ✗ in every other player's column. Every other card in their column gets ✗. They are completely out of the game, and their header shows 👋.

New

Tap New to start a new game. A pop-up appears — choose your players, choose your deck, then start:

  • Leaving the pop-up: Cancel or Close closes it, and the current game carries on untouched.
  • Players: Same Players keeps the seated names; New Players clears the seats.
  • Deck: Same Deck keeps the deck you shaped; New Deck returns to the default cards.
  • Starting: Start New Game wakes once both choices are made. Tap it twice to return to Setup and begin.
